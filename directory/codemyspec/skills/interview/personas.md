# Personas: who the product is for

Before stories, settle who the product's users are. A persona is one kind of
real person, described by what they do and want, that every story can name in
its "As a ...". Two to four is enough. One is the main one.

## Start with what the person already has

Ask by name for anything that already records their customers: interview or
call notes, support messages, reviews, sign-up answers, who pays most and who
needs the most help. Read what they hand you before asking them to describe
anyone. If they have nothing, work from what they know and say plainly that the
result is their best guess until real customers confirm it.

## What to find out, for each kind of person

- **What they are trying to get done** and what set them looking for a way.
- **What they do today instead**, and what is slow or painful about it.
- **How often, where and on what** they would use the product.
- **What would make them say no:** a feature they would refuse or ignore.
- **Who pays, if that is someone else.** A buyer and a user are two people.

Leave out age, gender, income and anything else that does not change what gets
built. Keep the person's own words where you have them.

## Checks before you move on

- Can you name a feature this person would reject? If not, the persona is too
  loose to settle anything.
- Would merging two of them change any story? If not, merge them.
- Is each claim something the person told you or showed you? Mark the rest as
  a guess, and ask what would confirm it.

Read the personas back and get agreement. Then use their names as the roles in
`workflow-mapping.md` and in each story.
